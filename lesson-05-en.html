Mass, Salt, and Heat Budgets

Session 5 · 2026-10-22 · Textbook Ch.5 §5.1–5.8

Driving question: How can you separate local forcing from ocean transport when a region warms or becomes saltier?

Class notes

A budget starts with a control volume, boundaries, and a sign convention: storage change equals net input plus internal sources and sinks. Volume transport is the velocity integrated across a section; surface velocity cannot represent the full depth.

Air–sea heat exchange has four terms: shortwave \(Q_s\), longwave \(Q_b\), latent \(Q_e\), and sensible \(Q_h\). With positive into the ocean, evaporative latent heat is usually a negative term.

Mixed-layer temperature also responds to advection, entrainment, and mixing; shortwave that passes through the layer is not all absorbed within it. Ignoring these terms gives \(\rho _{0}c_{p}h dT/dt\approx Q\). Freshwater exchange changes salinity and buoyancy; wind stress adds momentum and can deepen the mixed layer.

  • Define the control volume, boundaries, units, and signs first.
  • Surface fluxes and boundary transports both change storage.
  • Strong wind can increase evaporation and deepen the mixed layer at the same time; the net temperature change depends on the size of each term.

In my own words

I used to think all heat fluxes went from the ocean to the atmosphere. Later I saw that shortwave comes from the Sun into the ocean, while longwave, latent, and sensible heat are usually upward losses. One wrong sign reverses the whole budget.

What I did not understand + How I worked it out

What I did not understand at first: if net heat flux is downward (into the ocean), why can SST still fall?
What I later worked out: surface heating is only one term; cold-water advection, entrainment, and mixed-layer deepening can spread or remove the heat.
