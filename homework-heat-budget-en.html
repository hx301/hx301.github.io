All fluxes are positive into the ocean: \(Q_{\mathrm{net}} = Q_s + Q_b + Q_e + Q_h\). Upward heat losses are normally negative. Exact extrema in question 2 await the assigned course map.

1. What controls each heat-flux term?

Original question

What factors determine the heat budget terms, \(Q_s\), \(Q_b\), \(Q_e\) and \(Q_h\), respectively? Make clear the importance of each factor of each term.

Reference explanation

Take every flux as positive downward: \(Q_{\mathrm{net}} = Q_s + Q_b + Q_e + Q_h\). Shortwave depends on solar geometry, clouds and albedo; net longwave on sea and atmospheric temperature, humidity and clouds. Latent and sensible fluxes depend mainly on wind and the air–sea humidity and temperature contrasts, respectively.

Sources: [1], [3].

2. How large are the fluxes, and where are their extrema?

Original question

What are the ranges of the magnitude of the four terms? For each term, please specify the location and value of the maximum and minimum center.

Reference explanation

Typical annual-mean scales are \(10^{2} W m^{-2}\) for shortwave, \(10^{1}\text{–}10^{2}\) for latent heat loss, and 10¹ for net longwave and sensible heat loss. These are scales, not global extrema. Exact maxima, minima and locations require the assigned map; with downward-positive signs, strongest cooling means the most negative value.

Sources: [1], [3].
