Data Analysis and Observing Methods

Session 8 · 2026-11-05 · Textbook Ch.6 §6.1–6.7

Driving question: How can an ocean analysis be understood, checked, and reproduced by someone else?

Class notes

Analysis begins with sampling and quality control: precision describes the spread of repeated measurements, accuracy describes closeness to the true value; smooth numbers do not imply the absence of systematic bias. Insufficient sampling can miss variability or alias it.

Check units, quality flags, sensor depth, missing-value codes, and sampling intervals before choosing averages, filters, or plots. EOF decomposes space–time data into spatial modes (EOFs) and time coefficients (PCs): \(X=U\Sigma V^{T}\), with variance fraction \(\frac{\lambda_i}{\sum_j\lambda_j}\).

Removing the seasonal cycle or trend, area weighting, and standardization change the object of analysis. Report valid samples, code, errors, and sensitivity so the result can be reproduced. EOF optimizes the reconstruction of sample variance; it does not test causal mechanisms.

  • Correlation is not causation.
  • EOF1 is not mechanism 1.
  • Missing-data choices change the representativeness of the sample.

In my own words

I used to think that if EOF1 explained 90% of the variance, it explained 90% of the physical causes. Later I saw that it is only the spatial mode that best reconstructs sample variance under a given variable, region, weighting, and preprocessing; causal attribution needs independent evidence.

What I did not understand + How I worked it out

What I did not understand at first: if one EOF analysis removes the trend and another keeps it, and their leading variance fractions differ, is the code wrong?
What I later worked out: preprocessing changes the input matrix and total variance. First align the domain, missing-data rules, weights, and scaling, then compare sensitivity.
