1. How deep can a mixed layer be?

Original question

How deep is a typical mixed layer if mixed by wind? How deep can it reach if driven by cooling?

Reference explanation

Wind stirring commonly mixes the upper tens of metres, roughly up to 100 m, depending on stratification. Cooling-driven convection often reaches tens to several hundred metres and can exceed 1,500 m at exceptional deep-convection sites.

Sources: [2].

2. Temperature and salinity in the North Pacific

Original question

What are the typical vertical temperature and salinity profiles in the subtropical and subpolar regions of the North Pacific?

Reference explanation

The subtropical North Pacific usually has warm, saline surface water, a pronounced thermocline and an intermediate salinity minimum. The subpolar ocean has fresher surface water and a strong halocline; remnant winter water can produce a subsurface temperature minimum and inversion while density remains stably stratified.

Sources: [2], [7].

3. Where does evaporation exceed precipitation?

Original question

What are the dominant regions of net evaporation in the ocean?

Reference explanation

Net evaporation occurs mainly in the dry subtropics and strongly evaporative marginal seas. Rain belts and many high-latitude regions instead receive net precipitation. Salinity also depends on runoff, ice processes and ocean transport.

Sources: [1], [7].

4. The mixed-layer heat budget

Original question

What are the key components of the Mixed Layer heat budget?

Reference explanation

Mixed-layer temperature changes through absorbed surface heat, horizontal advection, entrainment and mixing. Subtract shortwave transmitted below the layer from \(Q_{\mathrm{net}}\). Entrainment cools a layer when underlying water is colder, but can warm it under a temperature inversion; a shallower layer responds more rapidly to a given heat input.

Sources: [4].

5. Why is the Atlantic saltier?

Original question

Is the North Atlantic Ocean saltier or fresher on average than the Pacific? Why?

Reference explanation

The North Atlantic is generally saltier because basin freshwater budgets and transport differ. Atmospheric moisture export toward the Pacific and northward ocean transport of saline subtropical water are important; Mediterranean outflow also affects intermediate depths. The contrast is not explained by evaporation alone.

Sources: [5], [7].

6. Summer-to-winter differences

Original question

What regions of the ocean are characterized by large differences from summer to winter? What regions have the least seasonal variability?

Reference explanation

Specify the variable first. SST seasonality is commonly stronger at midlatitudes and weaker in tropical warm pools or waters constrained near freezing. Mixed-layer-depth seasonality follows a different pattern, with large deepening in winter-convection regions. Exact extrema require a defined metric and dataset.

Sources: [1], [2].

7. What maintains a thermocline?

Original question

Please describe the two processes that maintain the thermocline.

Reference explanation

For a seasonal thermocline, discuss stabilizing surface heating versus mixing and cooling. For a permanent thermocline, a simple model balances downward heat diffusion with cold-water upwelling, while ventilation and three-dimensional advection also matter. The intended pair of processes requires the course context.

Sources: [2], [6].
